      ## June 2026 - Clarkson University Rocketry (CUR) returned to IREC for the
      third time in 2026, with our flight vehicle Aurora again competing in the
      10,000-foot commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) category.

      Concurrently, the avionics and recovery subteam team began development of
      our SRAD flight computer, laying the critical groundwork for custom
      avionics that will enable more complex flight profiles in coming years,
      and our flight computer boards were fabricated and assembled in the U.S.
      by our Gold Sponsor Sierra Circuits using their Turnkey PRO.


      For the recovery system, the team experimented with a single-separation,
      dual-deploy configuration and nested the avionics bay directly into the
      nose cone to optimize the vehicle layout and weight distribution for
      stability.

      Aurora was also designed to carry a scientific payload evaluating the
      effectiveness of various materials including carbon fiber, Kevlar, barium
      sulfate, and PEEK for shielding against high-altitude gamma radiation.


      These experimental payload data collection electronics were implemented on
      custom PCBs made possible by Gold Sponsor NextPCB and were secured into
      machined aluminum containers stacked in a central tower configuration.

    text: >-
      The team flew Aurora just before 7:00 PM on June 17, 2026 after preparing
      all systems and finishing integration of the rocket approximately 12 hours
      before. Just before launch, we suffered from telemetry connection issues,
      losing connection to the COTS flight computer during launch. The team
      observed a recovery event after apogee and noticed a section of the rocket
      drifting towards the horizon, while our live video system indicated that
      the booster separated from the rest of the rocket. As a result of this
      off-nominal recovery sequence, the team was unable to locate or recover
      any sections of the rocket, resulting in a disappointing placement of 133
      out of 141 teams participating in the 2026 IREC.


      The team has identified the primary flight computer and recovery system
      complexity as flight failure modes and initiated a GPS tracker R&D project
      allowing for multiple sections of the rocket to be located even if
      unintentionally separated.
